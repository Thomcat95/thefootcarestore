Emerging & Evidence-Based Therapies

These are newer options patients ask me about often. I've included what the current research actually shows — not just marketing claims — so you can make an informed decision alongside your usual care, not instead of it.

FDA-cleared adjunct

Red Light Therapy Device (Photobiomodulation)

The evidence here is mixed but leans positive. Several randomized trials and meta-analyses have found red light therapy improves short-term pain and, in at least one imaging-based study, measurably reduced plantar fascia thickness. That said, at least one more recent meta-analysis pooling similar data found it didn't consistently improve overall pain or function — so this isn't a settled question, and results likely vary by device and protocol. If you try it, think of it as an adjunct alongside stretching and your usual care, not a replacement for it. One distinction worth knowing: the FDA clearance behind the strongest research applies to specific clinical-use lasers, not any particular consumer device — most home devices carry a more general clearance for muscle/joint pain and circulation. Look for a device using red and near-infrared wavelengths in the range that's actually been studied (roughly 630–660nm and 810–850nm).

Early-stage evidence

PEMF (Pulsed Electromagnetic Field) Device

Used by some patients for pain and swelling, but the research specific to foot and ankle conditions is more limited and mixed than for red light therapy. Don't use if you have a pacemaker or other implanted electronic device, and check with your physician first — this is one to discuss before trying, not an established first-line option.
