Tactical Boots

Combat and tactical boots carry heavy loads over rough ground for long hours, which puts the feet at risk for blisters, stress fractures and Achilles and plantar fascia strain. These makers build boots for U.S. military, law enforcement and serious outdoor use. Here is what the military standards mean, followed by the boots.

Military standards explained
Made in USA

Berry Amendment

Federal law (10 U.S.C. 4862) requires boots bought by the Defense Department to be made in the United States from U.S. materials. "Berry compliant" boots meet that rule.

Army uniform

AR 670-1

The Army's uniform rule for optional combat boots calls for coyote brown, 8 to 10 inches high, cattlehide leather and/or nylon uppers, a plain toe, and a rubber or polyether polyurethane outsole. Unit rules can be stricter.

Military specification

MIL-SPEC boots

Issue boots are built to detailed military specifications, such as MIL-DTL-32237 for the Army hot weather combat boot, which set materials, construction and testing.

Other branches

Branch approvals

The Marine Corps certifies boots for uniform wear (EGA-marked), and the Navy publishes an approved boot list for its working uniform. Check your branch's current list.
