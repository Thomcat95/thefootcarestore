Shoe Fit Guide

Many foot problems seen in clinic start with shoes that are the wrong length, width or shape. Foot size changes with age, pregnancy, weight and swelling, so it's worth measuring again before your next pair.

At home

How to measure your feet

  1. Measure late in the day

    Feet swell during the day. Measuring in the late afternoon or evening, after you've been on your feet, matches how shoes will feel at the end of a long day.

  2. Stand on paper and trace both feet

    Wear the socks you'll wear with the shoes. Stand with your full weight on a sheet of paper against a wall and trace around each foot with the pen held straight up.

  3. Measure length and width

    Measure from the back of the heel to the tip of the longest toe, which is not always the big toe. Then measure across the widest part of the forefoot. Write both down for each foot.

  4. Use the larger foot

    Most people have one foot slightly larger. Size to the larger foot and add an insert or tongue pad to the other if needed.

  5. Check the brand's size chart

    Sizes vary between brands. Compare your measurements with each brand's chart rather than assuming last year's size.

In the shoe

What a good fit feels like

Toe room

Leave about a thumb's width (3/8 to 1/2 inch) between the longest toe and the end of the shoe, and room to wiggle the toes.

Widest part matches

The ball of the foot should sit at the widest part of the shoe, and the shoe should flex where your foot bends.

Heel stays put

Little to no heel slip in laced shoes. Pull-on western boots are the exception: a small amount of heel slip is normal until the sole breaks in.

No pressure points

No pinching across the bunion or the tops of the toes. Shoes should feel comfortable in the store. Don't count on them stretching.

Inserts

Fitting shoes around orthotics

  1. Bring the orthotic

    Try shoes on with the orthotic or insole you'll actually wear.

  2. Take the factory insole out

    Orthotics replace the shoe's insole. They don't go on top of it. Look for shoes with a removable insole.

  3. Check the heel and toes

    The heel shouldn't lift out when you walk, and the toes shouldn't press on the top of the shoe. If they do, look for extra-depth styles.

Leather boots

Breaking in leather and handmade boots

  1. Start indoors

    Wear new boots around the house for an hour or two a day for the first week, with the socks you'll wear outside.

  2. Expect the insole to settle

    A leather insole over cork molds to the foot over two to four weeks of wear. Boots that feel snug across the instep at first often ease as it settles.

  3. Protect hot spots

    Cover rubbing areas early with moleskin or a blister bandage instead of pushing through. Stop if a blister forms.

  4. Know the limits

    Leather gives a little in width, but not in length. If your toes touch the end, the boot is too short.

Replace

When to replace athletic shoes

Every 300 to 500 miles of running or walking, or sooner if the midsole is creased, the heel is worn unevenly, or the shoe twists easily.

Children

Check children's sizes often

Young children can outgrow shoes every few months. Measure every 3 to 4 months, and don't pass down worn shoes that have taken the shape of another child's foot.

See a podiatrist

When shoes aren't enough

See a podiatrist for foot pain that lasts more than two weeks, numbness or tingling, or any sore on the foot if you have diabetes.
