Footwear · How-to

Shoe Fit Guide

Many foot problems start with shoes that are the wrong length, width or shape. Foot size changes with age, pregnancy, weight and swelling, so it's worth measuring again before your next pair.

At home

How to Measure Your Feet

Five steps, about five minutes.

Step 1

Measure late in the day

Feet swell during the day. Measuring in the late afternoon or evening matches how shoes will feel at the end of a long day.

Step 2

Trace both feet

Wear the socks you'll wear with the shoes. Stand with your full weight on paper against a wall and trace each foot with the pen held straight up.

Step 3

Measure length and width

Measure from the back of the heel to the tip of the longest toe, which isn't always the big toe, then across the widest part of the forefoot.

Step 4

Use the larger foot

Most people have one foot slightly larger. Size to the larger foot and add an insert or tongue pad to the other if needed.

Step 5

Check the brand's chart

Sizes vary between brands. Compare your measurements with each brand's size chart rather than assuming last year's size.

In the shoe

What a Good Fit Feels Like

Shoes should feel comfortable in the store. Don't count on them stretching.

Toe room

A thumb's width

Leave about 3/8 to 1/2 inch between the longest toe and the end of the shoe, with room to wiggle the toes.

Ball of foot

Widest part matches

The ball of the foot should sit at the widest part of the shoe, and the shoe should flex where your foot bends.

Heel

Heel stays put

Little to no heel slip in laced shoes. Pull-on western boots are the exception: a small amount of slip is normal until the sole breaks in.

Pressure

No pinching

No pressure across a bunion or the tops of the toes.

Inserts

Fitting Shoes Around Orthotics

Orthotics replace the shoe's insole. They don't go on top of it.

Step 1

Bring the orthotic

Try shoes on with the orthotic or insole you'll actually wear.

Step 2

Remove the factory insole

Look for shoes with a removable insole so the orthotic can take its place.

Step 3

Check heel and toes

The heel shouldn't lift out when you walk, and the toes shouldn't press on the top of the shoe. If they do, look for extra-depth styles.

Leather boots

Breaking In Leather and Handmade Boots

A leather insole over cork molds to the foot over two to four weeks of wear.

Week 1

Start indoors

Wear new boots around the house for an hour or two a day, with the socks you'll wear outside.

Weeks 2 to 4

Let the insole settle

Boots that feel snug across the instep at first often ease as the insole settles.

Any time

Protect hot spots

Cover rubbing areas early with moleskin or a blister bandage. Stop if a blister forms.

Know the limits

Length doesn't stretch

Leather gives a little in width but not in length. If your toes touch the end, the boot is too short.

Good to know

When to Replace Shoes and When to See a Podiatrist

Replace

Athletic shoes

Every 300 to 500 miles of running or walking, or sooner if the midsole is creased, the heel is worn unevenly, or the shoe twists easily.

Children

Check sizes often

Young children can outgrow shoes every few months. Measure every 3 to 4 months, and avoid hand-me-downs that have taken the shape of another child's foot.

See a podiatrist

When shoes aren't enough

Foot pain that lasts more than two weeks, numbness or tingling, or any sore on the foot if you have diabetes.
