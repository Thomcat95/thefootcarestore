Luxury & Handmade Shoes and Boots

A higher price can buy better construction: a sole that can be replaced, a leather insole that molds to the foot, and a heel that holds its shape. It can also buy just a name. This page explains how to tell the difference.

Construction

Inside a Goodyear-Welted Boot

Most shoes have the sole glued on (cemented), and when it wears through, the shoe is done. A welted shoe sews a leather strip, the welt, to the upper and insole, then stitches the outsole to the welt, so a cobbler can replace the sole many times.

Cross-section across the width of a Goodyear-welted boot. Stitchdown and Blake-stitched shoes can also be resoled, by different methods.

Buyer's checklist

What to Look For

Check the product description and the shoe itself for these.

Lifespan

A welted or stitchdown sole

Look for "Goodyear welt," "stitchdown" or "Blake stitch" in the description. A visible row of stitches around the top edge of the sole is a good sign. "Cemented" means glued.

Fit over time

Leather insole over cork

A full leather insole on a cork bed slowly molds to the shape of the foot over the first weeks of wear.

Midfoot support

A shank

A strip of steel, fiberglass or leather under the arch keeps the midfoot from collapsing, especially in heeled and western boots.

Heel control

A firm heel counter

Squeeze the back of the heel. It should hold its shape. A firm counter keeps the heel centered, which helps with flat feet, ankle instability and heel pain.

Toe room

The right last

The last is the foot-shaped form a shoe is built on, and it sets the toe shape. Choose a rounded or wide last for bunions or hammertoes, not a narrow pointed one.

True fit

Widths and half sizes

Better makers offer several widths, so a wide foot doesn't have to size up in length. That keeps the arch and flex point in the right place.

Comfort

Full leather lining

Leather linings breathe and wick moisture better than synthetic ones, which helps with sweaty feet, odor and blisters.

Orthotic users

Check the insole

Many handmade boots have a fixed leather insole. If you wear custom orthotics, ask whether there's room for one, and bring it to the fitting.

Custom

Custom and Made-to-Measure Footwear

Made-to-measure means the maker picks or adjusts a last to your measurements. Fully custom (bespoke) means a last is carved for your feet alone. Expect measurements or a foot tracing, a fitting, and a wait of weeks to months.

Who benefits

Feet that ready-made shoes don't fit

People whose feet differ by more than half a size, very wide or narrow feet, high insteps, or toe shapes that standard lasts don't fit.

Medical needs

See a podiatrist first

For diabetes with neuropathy, a past foot ulcer, or deformity after surgery, medical-grade custom or extra-depth shoes may be the safer choice. Some insurance plans, including Medicare's therapeutic shoe benefit for diabetes, may help pay for them.

Break-in

Allow a few weeks

Even custom shoes need a few weeks for the leather insole to settle. See the break-in guide.

Makers

Handmade, Heritage and Luxury Makers

Construction varies by model, so check the product description for the welt type and insole before buying.
